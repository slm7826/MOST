\section{Basic equations of motion}

Following \cite{Monin1971}, start with the basic equations of motion in Boussinesq approximation:
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align} \label{eq:boussinesq}
   \pderiv{\bm{u}}{t} + (\bm{u} \cdot \nabla) \bm{u} & = -\frac{1}{\rho_0}\nabla{p'} + \nu \nabla^2 \bm{u} - \frac{\bm{g}}{p_0}\pt' \\
   \pderiv{\pt}{t} + (\bm{u} \cdot \nabla) \bm{\pt} & =  \chi\nabla^2 \pt
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
and continuity equation in approximation of incompressible liquid
\begin{equation}
   \nabla \cdot \bm{u} = 0
\end{equation}
where $\pt$ is potential temperature, $\pt'$ is the departure from mean, and $p'$ is the departure from hydrostatics.
Viscosity terms in the right-hand side are typically neglected.

Fluxes of momentum and heat within constant flux layer
\begin{gather}
   \tau = \rho_0 (\overline{u'w'}) = \text{const}_1 \\
   H = \rho_0 c_p (\overline{\pt'w'}) = \text{const}_2
\end{gather}

Friction velocity scale $\ustar$ is defined by the momentum sink at the surface,
\begin{equation}
   \tau = \rho_0 (\overline{u'w'}) = \rho_0 \ustar^2
\end{equation}

Similarly, temperature scale $\tstar$ is defined by the relationship
\begin{equation}
   H = \rho_0 c_p (\overline{\pt'w'}) = \rho_0 c_p \tstar \ustar
\end{equation}

\section{Dimensional reasoning}

\subsection{Neutral case}
In the neutral case, far from the wall (where the viscosity effects can be important) the vertical gradient of the mean wind can only depend on stress $\tau$, air density $\rho$, and vertical coordinate $z$ \cite[see][section 5.3]{Monin1971}, therefore the equation becomes:
\begin{equation}
   \pderiv{u}{z} = \frac{\ustar}{\vonkarman z}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stratified flow}
The statistics of the flow in the near-surface layer can only depend on small number of parameters that appear in the above section, namely on temperature-to-buoyancy scale $g/\pt_0$, density $\rho_0$, fluxes of momentum $\tau$ (or, equivalently, velocity scale $\ustar$) and temperature $H/(\rho_0 c_p)=\overline{\pt'w'}$, and $z$. Units of these quantities are
\begin{equation}
    \left[\frac{g}{\pt_0}\right] = \si{\per\K\m\per\s\squared}; \quad
    \left[\rho_0\right] = \si{\kg\per\m\cubed}; \quad
    \left[\ustar\right] = \si{\m\per\s}; \quad
    \left[\overline{\pt'w'}\right] = \si{\K\m\per\s}
\end{equation}

There is only one way (to the arbitrary power) to combine the above parameters to construct the values of length dimension:
\begin{equation}
    \left[\frac{g}{\pt_0}\right]^{n_1}
    \left[\rho_0\right]^{n_2}
    \left[\ustar\right]^{n_3}
    \left[\overline{\pt'w'}\right]^{n_4} = [z]
\end{equation}
and that is
\begin{equation}
   L = \frac{\ustar^3}{\vonkarman\frac{g}{\pt_0}(\overline{\pt'w'})}
\end{equation}

Per \cite{Monin1971}, the dimensionless von Karman constant $\vonkarman$ was first introduced into the equation for $L$ by Obukhov in 1946 and has usually been preserved in all subsequent works by tradition. $L>0$ for stable situations; $L<0$ for unstable cases.

In non-neutral case,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:u-profile}
   \frac{\vonkarman z}{\ustar}\pderiv{u}{z} = \fstab_m(\zeta)
\end{equation}
where
$\zeta=z/L$,
$\fstab_m$ is stability correction function,
$\fstab_m \to 1$ as $\zeta \to 0$ ro retrieve neutral case.

\cite{Held2004} then defines
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:Held-integral}
\begin{align}
   F_m(\zeta) & = \int\frac{\fstab_m}{\zeta}\,d \zeta \\
   F_h(\zeta) & = \int\frac{\fstab_h}{\zeta}\,d \zeta
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
to solve \eqref{eq:u-profile} as
\begin{equation}
   u(z) = \frac{\ustar}{\vonkarman} \left[
          F_m\left(\frac{z}{L}\right)-F_m\left(\frac{\zm}{L}\right)
      \right]
\end{equation}

Other literature, including \cite{Jimenez2012} use equivalent approach to define
\begin{equation}
   \psi_m(\zeta) = \int\frac{1-\fstab_m(\zeta)}{\zeta}\, d \zeta
               = \ln(\zeta) - F_m(\zeta)
\end{equation}
to get
\begin{equation}
   u(z) = \frac{\ustar}{\vonkarman}\left[
          \ln\left(\frac{z}{\zm}\right) - \psi_m\left(\frac{z}{L}\right) + \psi_m\left(\frac{\zm}{L}\right)
      \right]
\end{equation}

Similarly, for temperature
\begin{equation}\label{eq:b-profile}
   \frac{\vonkarman z}{\tstar}\pderiv{\pt}{z} = \fstab_h(\zeta)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
   \pt(z) - \pt(0) = \frac{\tstar}{\vonkarman} \left[
          F_h\left(\frac{z}{L}\right)-F_h\left(\frac{\zh}{L}\right)
      \right]
\end{equation}
or
\begin{equation}
   \pt(z) - \pt(0) = \frac{\tstar}{\vonkarman}\left[
          \ln\left(\frac{z}{\zh}\right) - \psi_h\left(\frac{z}{L}\right) + \psi_h\left(\frac{\zh}{L}\right)
      \right]
\end{equation}

\subsection{Turbulent diffusion coefficients}
By definition of turbulent diffusion coefficient
\begin{equation}
   (\overline{u'w'}) = K_m \pderiv{u}{z} = \ustar^2
\end{equation}
and substituting ${\partial u/\partial z}$ from~\eqref{eq:u-profile}
\begin{equation}
   K_m = \frac{\ustar^2}{\partial u/\partial z} = \frac{\ustar \vonkarman z}{\fstab_m(\zeta)}
\end{equation}

\section{Stability functions}
Current FMS code (2020.04) can use a number of stability correction functions, controlled by options specified in \verb|monin_obukhov_nml|.

\subsection{Stable option 1}
In unstable regime ($\zeta < 0$), the stability functions are defined as
\begin{subequations}
\label{eq:fms-unstable}
\begin{align}
   \fstab_m(\zeta) & = (1-16 \zeta)^{-1/4} \\
   \fstab_h(\zeta) & = \begin{cases}
      (1-16\zeta)^{-1/3}, & \text{for new option (Ming Zhao)} \\
      (1-16\zeta)^{-1/2}, & \text{for old option}
   \end{cases}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
Note that the momentum stability function is the same as in \cite{Held2004}

In stable case (for $\zeta>0$)
\begin{equation}
   \fstab_m(\zeta) = \fstab_h(\zeta) = 1 + \left(5 + \frac{\zeta}{\rcrit}\right)\frac{\zeta}{1+\zeta} \\
\end{equation}

\subsection{Stable option 2}
In unstable regime, stability corrections are the same as in stable option 1, equations~\eqref{eq:fms-unstable}. In stable regime
\begin{equation}
   \fstab_m(\zeta) = \fstab_h(\zeta) = \begin{cases}
      1+5\zeta, & \zeta < \zeta_t \\
      1+5 \zeta_t + \frac{\zeta-\zeta_t}{\rcrit}, & \zeta \ge \zeta_t
   \end{cases}
\end{equation}

Note that in stable case, $\fstab_m(\zeta) = \fstab_h(\zeta)$, like they were in \cite{Held2004}.

\subsection{Brutsaert}
Stability function proposed in \cite{Brutsaert2005} was introduced in the FMS code on branch \verb|usr/slm/sfcx|, together with refactoring of the Monin-Obukhov code to make it more easily expandable and manageable.

For unstable case
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
    \fstab_m(\zeta) & = \begin{cases}
        (a_u+b_u y^{4/3})/(a_u + y), & y \le b_u^{-3} \\
        1,                            & y > b_u^{-3}
    \end{cases}
    \\
    \fstab_h(\zeta) & = (c_u + d_u y^{n_u})/(c_u+y^{n_u})
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
where $y=-\zeta$, $a_u = 0.33$, $b_u = 0.41$, $c_u = 0.33$, $d_u = 0.057$, and $n_u = 0.78$.

In stable case
\begin{equation}
    \fstab_m(\zeta) = \fstab_h(\zeta)
        = 1 + a_s \frac{\zeta + \zeta^{b_s}(1+\zeta^{b_s})^{-1+1/b_s}}
                       {\zeta + (1+\zeta^{b_s})^{1/b_s}}
\end{equation}
with $a_s = 6.1$ and  $b_s = 2.5$.

The complicated form of stable case correction $\fstab_x$ is because a relatively simple integral stability function ws postulated first
\begin{equation}
\Psi_x = -a_s \ln\big[\zeta + (1+\zeta^{b_s})^{1/b_s}\big]
\end{equation}
and then then stability corrections $\fstab_x$ were obtained by differentiation.

\section{Solving for surface fluxes}
Following \cite{Held2004} note, bulk Richardson number can be calculated from known winds and virtual potential temperatures at the top of the surface boundary layer $z_a$ and the surface as
\begin{equation}
    \rbulk = z_a\frac{b(z_a)-b(0)}{u(z_a)^2} = g z_a \frac{\theta_v(z_a)-\theta_v(0)}{\theta_v(0)u(z_a)^2}
\end{equation}
where
$z_a$ is the height of the height of the lowest atmospheric layer, and  $\zeta_a = z_a/L$.

On the other hand, the same bulk Richardson function can be expressed in terms of integral stability functions~\eqref{eq:Held-integral}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ribulk}
    \rbulk = \zeta \frac{F_t(\zeta_a) - F_t(\zeta_a \zh/z_a)}
        {(F_m(\zeta_a) - F_m(\zeta_a \zm/z_a))^2}
\end{equation}
where
$\zm$ is the roughness length of momentum,
$\zh$ -- roughness length for buoyancy,
and
$\zeta_a = z_a/L$.

Defining, for convenience, function
\begin{equation}
    F_i(\zeta_1, \zeta_2) = F_i(\zeta_2) - F_i(\zeta_1)
                          = \int_{\zeta_1}^{\zeta_2} \frac{\fstab_i(\zeta)}{\zeta} d \zeta
\end{equation}
we can rewrite~\eqref{eq:ribulk} as
\begin{equation}\label{eqzeta}
    f(\zeta_a) = \zeta_a \frac{F_t(\zeta_a \zh/z_a, \zeta_a)}{F_m(\zeta_a \zm/z_a,\zeta_a)^2} - \rbulk = 0
\end{equation}

FMS code solves nonlinear equation~\eqref{eqzeta} iteratively, to find the value of $\zeta_a = z_a/L$ that match boundary conditions. The solution procedure follows the Newton method, so on each iteration step, given a guess of $\zeta_0$, the step $\Delta \zeta$ is calculated as
\begin{equation}
   f(\zeta_0) + \fderiv{f}{\zeta} \Delta \zeta = 0
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
   \Delta \zeta = - f(\zeta_0)\Big[\fderiv{f}{\zeta}\Big]^{-1}
\end{equation}
until its absolute value falls below predefined tolerance.

Noting that
\begin{equation}
    \pderiv{F_i(\zeta_1,\zeta_2)}{\zeta_1} = - \frac{\fstab_i(\zeta_1)}{\zeta_1}, \qquad
    \pderiv{F_i(\zeta_1,\zeta_2)}{\zeta_2} = \frac{\fstab_i(\zeta_2)}{\zeta_2}
\end{equation}
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
\fderiv{f}{\zeta}
    & =  \frac{F_t(\zeta \zh/z_a, \zeta)}{F_m(\zeta \zm/z_a,\zeta)^2}
    + \frac{\fstab_h(\zeta)-\fstab_h(\zeta \zh/z_a)}{F_m(\zeta \zm/z_a,\zeta)^2}
    - 2 \frac{F_t(\zeta \zh/z_a, \zeta)}{F_m(\zeta \zm/z_a,\zeta)^3}(\fstab_m(\zeta)-\fstab_m(\zeta \zm/z_a))
    \label{eq:Fderiv1}
    \\
    {} &=  \zeta\frac{F_t(\zeta \zh/z_a, \zeta)}{F_m(\zeta \zm/z_a,\zeta)^2}
        \Bigg[
            \frac{1}{\zeta}
            + \frac{\fstab_h(\zeta)-\fstab_h(\zeta \zh/z_a)}{\zeta F_t(\zeta \zh/z_a,\zeta)}
            - 2 \frac{\fstab_m(\zeta)-\fstab_m(\zeta \zm/z_a)}{\zeta F_m(\zeta \zm/z_a,\zeta)}
        \Bigg]
    \label{eq:Fderiv2}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

\begin{mdframed}
An alternative mathematically equivalent form of equation~\eqref{eqzeta} would be
\begin{equation}
    f_1(\zeta) = \zeta F_t(\zeta \zh/z_a, \zeta) - \rbulk F_m(\zeta \zm/z_a,\zeta)^2 = 0
\end{equation}
so that the derivative of the function would be simpler:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \fderiv{f_1}{\zeta} & = F_t(\zeta \zh/z_a, \zeta) + \fstab_h(\zeta)-\fstab_h(\zeta \zh/z_a) \\
    & \qquad \qquad - 2 \rbulk F_m(\zeta \zm/z_a,\zeta) \frac{1}{\zeta} [\fstab_m(\zeta)-\fstab_m(\zeta \zm/z_a)]
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
This is not the expression that the code is using, though. Not sure why, it looks simpler.
The only disadvantage that I can see is that it includes $1/\zeta$ term that may cause problems at $\zeta=0$; the equation~\eqref{eq:Fderiv1} does not, although the code uses the form~\eqref{eq:Fderiv2}.
\end{mdframed}

\section{Free convection limit}

As $u \to 0$, $\ustar \to 0$, and $\zeta \to -\infty$. In this case, vertical profile of temperature may become ill defined, depending on the asymptotic properties of the stability functions. Consequently, vertical flux of buoyancy $B$ becomes ill defined.

To see what is going on, start from equation for vertical profile of buoyancy \eqref{eq:b-profile}:
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{\vonkarman z}{\bstar}\pderiv{b}{z} = \fstab_h(\zeta)
\end{equation*}
Also, from definition of \bstar~\eqref{eq:bstar}
\begin{equation*}
    \bstar = \frac{B}{\rho \ustar}
\end{equation*}
and definition of $\zeta$
\begin{equation*}
    \zeta = \frac{z}{L} = -\frac{z \vonkarman \bstar}{\ustar^2}
\end{equation*}
we get
\begin{equation}
    \pderiv{b}{z} = \frac{\bstar}{\vonkarman z}\fstab_h(\zeta)
    = \frac{\bstar}{\vonkarman z}\fstab_h\left(-\frac{z \vonkarman \bstar}{\ustar^2}\right)
    = \frac{B}{\vonkarman z \rho \ustar }\fstab_h
      \left(-\frac{z \vonkarman B}{\rho \ustar^3}\right)
\end{equation}

When the wind is close to zero in non-neutral situation, it is reasonable to assume that the heat flux should not depend on $\ustar$. Therefore, in equation~\eqref{eq:b-profile}, the dependence on $\ustar$ should disappear when wind goes to zero (and, therefore, $\ustar \to 0$ and $\zeta \to \infty$). Suppose $\fstab(\zeta) \propto \zeta^n$ for $\zeta \to -\infty$, with some power $n$. Then
\begin{equation}
    \frac{B}{\vonkarman z \rho \ustar }\fstab_h \left(-\frac{z \vonkarman B}{\rho \ustar^3}\right)
  = \frac{B}{\vonkarman z \rho \ustar } C \left(-\frac{z \vonkarman B}{\rho \ustar^3}\right)^n
  = - C \frac{B^{n+1} z^{n-1} \vonkarman^{n-1}}{\rho^{n+1}} \frac{1}{\ustar^{3n+1}}
\end{equation}
For this expression to be finite and non-zero when $\ustar \to 0$, we should have $n=-1/3$.

For $n < -1/3$, the vertical gradient of buoyancy would tend to zero for finite heat flux $B$, which implies that the heat flux would tend to infinity for finite values of vertical gradient. On the other hand, if $n > -1/3$, the vertical gradient would tend to infinity for finite $B$.

\cite{Held2004} and \cite{Beljaars1994} propose to use ``gustiness'' to solve this problem: adding an ortogonal component to the atmospheric wind speed, determined by the $\bstar$, $\ustar$, and imposed parameters of the convective boundary layer:
\begin{gather}
   |U| = U^2 + V^2 + G^2 \\
   G = \beta \left(z_i \bstar \ustar \right)^{1/3}
\end{gather}
where $\beta$ is an empirical constant on the order of 1, and
$z_i$ is the characteristic height of the convective boundary layer \citep{Beljaars1994}. In GFDL models, $z_i$ is assumed to be constant \SI{1000}{m}.

This treatment should ensure the same asymptotic behavior as imposing the above constraint on $\fstab_h$ at $\ustar \to -\infty$. However, such approach can only work if calculation of gustiness is embedded within the solution of the implicit equations of heat and momentum balances in the Monin-Obukhov layer. In GFDL model that is not done: rather, gustiness is calculated in the atmosphere based on the flux and stability values from the previous time step. Therefore, I believe there is no guarantee that the surface fluxes produce reasonable solution in convective limit.
